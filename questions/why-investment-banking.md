## What the interviewer is testing

Whether your reasons are specific and tied to your own experience, whether you understand the work and the hours, and whether the role fits a credible plan for what you want to do later.

## Answer framework

1. Pick two or three specific reasons tied to your experience.
2. Cover the type of work: transactions, analysis, client exposure.
3. Cover the learning curve.
4. Connect to what you want to do later.
5. Show you understand the hours and workload.

## Sample answer

> An outline to fill with your own experience: "Two experiences pointed me here. [Experience 1] showed me I enjoy [transaction work / analysis / client exposure]. [Experience 2] showed me [what you learned]. Banking is where I can do that work every day with a steep learning curve, and it builds toward [what you want to do later]. I have spoken with [people in the role] about the hours and workload, and I am prepared for them."
>
> Keep it specific and tied to what you have actually done. The same story-shaped approach works for "walk me through your resume": where you started, two or three choices that led you toward finance, and why this role is the logical next step.

## Likely follow-ups

**Why our bank (or group)?** Name concrete things: recent deals the group advised on, its focus areas, people you spoke to and what you learned from them, and how its deal mix fits your interests.

## Common mistakes

- Generic answers about money or prestige.
- Reasons that could apply to any job and are not tied to your experience.
- Not showing you understand the hours and workload.
- No link to what you want to do later.
