## What the interviewer is testing

Whether you can move a single change through all three statements in the right order, keep track of tax, and end with a balance sheet that balances.

## Answer framework

1. State the tax rate you are assuming (25% here).
2. Income statement: pre-tax income falls by the change; net income falls by the after-tax amount.
3. Cash flow statement: start with the lower net income, add back the non-cash depreciation; the net cash effect is the tax saving.
4. Balance sheet: cash changes by the tax saving, net PP&E falls by the depreciation, retained earnings fall by the net income change.
5. Check that both sides move by the same amount.

## Sample answer

> Assume a 25% tax rate. On the income statement, operating income and pre-tax income fall by $10, so net income falls by $7.50.
>
> On the cash flow statement, net income is down $7.50, but depreciation is a non-cash expense, so I add back $10. Cash is up $2.50, which is the tax saving.
>
> On the balance sheet, cash is up $2.50 and net PP&E is down $10, so total assets are down $7.50. Retained earnings fall by $7.50, so liabilities plus equity are also down $7.50. It balances.

## Likely follow-ups

**How does a $100 goodwill impairment that is not tax-deductible flow through?** Net income falls $100, the non-cash charge is added back so cash is unchanged, and goodwill and retained earnings each fall $100.

## Common mistakes

- Saying cash goes down. Depreciation does not use cash; the only cash effect is lower taxes.
- Forgetting tax, so net income falls by the full $10.
- Forgetting to reduce net PP&E, so the balance sheet does not balance.
- Not stating the tax rate up front.
