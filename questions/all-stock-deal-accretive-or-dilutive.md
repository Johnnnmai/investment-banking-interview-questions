<h2>What the interviewer is testing</h2><p>Whether you know the P/E rule for all-stock deals, why it works, and what can override it.</p>
<h2>Answer framework</h2><ol class=steps><li>Rule: if the buyer&#x27;s P/E is higher than the P/E it pays for the target, the deal is accretive; if lower, dilutive.</li><li>Why: the buyer issues shares priced at its own P/E and receives earnings priced at the purchase P/E.</li><li>Use the purchase price, including the premium, for the target&#x27;s P/E, not its pre-deal trading P/E.</li><li>Example: buyer at 20x, target bought at 15x: accretive before other adjustments.</li><li>Overrides: synergies, D&amp;A from write-ups and transaction costs can change the result.</li></ol>
<h2>Sample answer</h2><div class=sample><p class=ans>In an all-stock deal, it comes down to comparing P/E multiples. If the buyer&#x27;s P/E is higher than the P/E it is paying for the target, the deal is accretive. If the buyer&#x27;s P/E is lower, it is dilutive.</p><p class=ans>The intuition is that the buyer is using its stock as currency. A buyer trading at 20 times earnings effectively pays for each new dollar of earnings with shares valued at 20 times that buyer&#x27;s earnings, an earnings yield of 5%. If it acquires the target at 15 times earnings, it receives earnings with a 6.7% yield. Buying 6.7% earnings with 5% currency raises EPS. Reverse the numbers, a buyer at 12 times acquiring a target at 18 times, and EPS falls.</p><p class=ans>Two details matter. The target&#x27;s P/E must be based on the offer price including the premium, not its undisturbed share price. And the rule ignores other adjustments: synergies can turn a dilutive deal accretive, while extra amortization from writing up intangible assets and transaction costs can do the opposite. So I use the P/E rule as a quick answer and confirm with a full pro forma calculation.</p></div>
<h2>Likely follow-ups</h2><div class=fu><b>Does the size of the target change the answer?</b> Not the direction under the simple rule, but it changes the magnitude of accretion or dilution.</div>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2><ul class=mist><li>Using the target&#x27;s pre-deal trading P/E instead of the purchase P/E.</li><li>Reversing the rule.</li><li>Ignoring synergies and amortization from write-ups.</li><li>Applying the P/E rule to deals funded with cash or debt.</li></ul>
