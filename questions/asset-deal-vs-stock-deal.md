<h2>What the interviewer is testing</h2><p>Whether you understand the legal and tax differences between acquisition structures and why buyers and sellers prefer different ones.</p>
<h2>Answer framework</h2><ol class=steps><li>Stock deal: the buyer acquires the target&#x27;s shares and takes on all assets and liabilities, known and unknown.</li><li>Asset deal: the buyer acquires selected assets and chooses which liabilities to assume.</li><li>Tax: an asset deal steps up the tax basis of assets, giving tax-deductible depreciation and goodwill amortization (15 years in the US).</li><li>Seller view: a C-corp selling assets can face double taxation, so sellers usually prefer stock deals.</li><li>Middle ground: a Section 338(h)(10) election treats a stock purchase as an asset purchase for tax purposes in certain cases.</li></ol>
<h2>Sample answer</h2><div class=sample><p class=ans>In a stock deal, the buyer purchases the target company&#x27;s shares, so it gets the whole legal entity: all assets, contracts and liabilities, including unknown ones such as future lawsuits. It is simpler to execute because contracts and licenses usually stay with the company. In an asset deal, the buyer purchases specific assets and assumes only the liabilities it agrees to, which limits risk but can require transferring contracts and permits one by one.</p><p class=ans>The biggest difference is tax. In an asset deal, the buyer records the assets at the purchase price for tax purposes, a step-up in tax basis, so it gets higher tax-deductible depreciation, and in the US goodwill is amortized for tax over 15 years. That lowers future cash taxes. In a stock deal, the tax basis usually carries over; any book write-up creates a deferred tax liability instead.</p><p class=ans>Sellers often prefer stock deals. If a C-corporation sells its assets, the company pays tax on the gain and shareholders pay tax again when proceeds are distributed. So buyers tend to prefer asset deals and sellers stock deals, and the price reflects that. A Section 338(h)(10) election, available in certain cases such as buying a subsidiary or an S-corp, can give the buyer the tax step-up while legally buying stock.</p></div>
<h2>Likely follow-ups</h2><div class=fu><b>Why might a buyer pay more in an asset deal?</b> The tax step-up creates future tax savings, whose value can be shared with the seller through a higher price.</div>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2><ul class=mist><li>Saying the buyer takes on all liabilities in an asset deal.</li><li>Forgetting the tax basis step-up.</li><li>Ignoring double taxation for C-corp sellers.</li><li>Confusing book write-ups in stock deals with tax step-ups.</li></ul>
