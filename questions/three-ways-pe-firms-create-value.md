<h2>What the interviewer is testing</h2><p>Whether you can decompose LBO returns into their drivers and judge which ones are most reliable.</p>
<h2>Answer framework</h2><ol class=steps><li>EBITDA growth: revenue growth and margin improvement raise enterprise value at exit.</li><li>Multiple expansion: selling at a higher EV/EBITDA multiple than the entry multiple.</li><li>Debt paydown (deleveraging): free cash flow repays debt, so more of the enterprise value belongs to equity.</li><li>Leverage itself amplifies these effects on equity returns.</li><li>Reliability: EBITDA growth and paydown are more controllable; multiple expansion depends on markets.</li></ol>
<h2>Sample answer</h2><div class=sample><p class=ans>Returns in a leveraged buyout come from three levers. The first is EBITDA growth: increasing revenue through pricing, new products or add-on acquisitions, and improving margins through cost savings or better purchasing. If EBITDA grows from $100M to $130M and the exit multiple is unchanged, enterprise value grows by 30%.</p><p class=ans>The second is multiple expansion: selling at a higher multiple than you paid. That can happen because the company becomes larger, faster-growing or less risky, or simply because market conditions improve. Private equity firms usually do not count on it when they underwrite a deal, because it depends heavily on the market at exit.</p><p class=ans>The third is debt paydown. The company uses its free cash flow to repay the acquisition debt, so even if enterprise value stays flat, the equity&#x27;s share of that value grows. Leverage amplifies all three, because equity is a small share of the capital: a modest increase in enterprise value becomes a larger percentage gain on equity. When I evaluate a deal, I want most of the projected return to come from EBITDA growth and paydown, which the firm can influence, rather than from multiple expansion.</p></div>
<h2>Likely follow-ups</h2><div class=fu><b>Which lever is the most dependable?</b> Debt paydown and cost-driven EBITDA improvement, since the firm controls them more than exit market multiples.</div>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2><ul class=mist><li>Listing leverage as a lever without explaining how it amplifies the others.</li><li>Relying on multiple expansion to justify a deal.</li><li>Forgetting that paydown requires real free cash flow.</li><li>Confusing revenue growth with EBITDA growth.</li></ul>
