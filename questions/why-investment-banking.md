<h2>What the interviewer is testing</h2><p>Whether your reasons are specific and tied to your own experience, whether you understand the work and the hours, and whether the role fits a credible plan for what you want to do later.</p>
<h2>Answer framework</h2><ol class=steps><li>Pick two or three specific reasons tied to your experience.</li><li>Cover the type of work: transactions, analysis, client exposure.</li><li>Cover the learning curve.</li><li>Connect to what you want to do later.</li><li>Show you understand the hours and workload.</li></ol>
<h2>Sample answer</h2><div class=sample><p class=ans>An outline to fill with your own experience: &quot;Two experiences pointed me here. [Experience 1] showed me I enjoy [transaction work / analysis / client exposure]. [Experience 2] showed me [what you learned]. Banking is where I can do that work every day with a steep learning curve, and it builds toward [what you want to do later]. I have spoken with [people in the role] about the hours and workload, and I am prepared for them.&quot;</p><p class=ans>Keep it specific and tied to what you have actually done. The same story-shaped approach works for &quot;walk me through your resume&quot;: where you started, two or three choices that led you toward finance, and why this role is the logical next step.</p></div>
<h2>Likely follow-ups</h2><div class=fu><b>Why our bank (or group)?</b> Name concrete things: recent deals the group advised on, its focus areas, people you spoke to and what you learned from them, and how its deal mix fits your interests.</div>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2><ul class=mist><li>Generic answers about money or prestige.</li><li>Reasons that could apply to any job and are not tied to your experience.</li><li>Not showing you understand the hours and workload.</li><li>No link to what you want to do later.</li></ul>
