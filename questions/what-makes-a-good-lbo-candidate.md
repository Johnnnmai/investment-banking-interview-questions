<h2>What the interviewer is testing</h2><p>Whether you can link each characteristic to how an LBO generates returns, particularly the ability to carry and repay debt.</p>
<h2>Answer framework</h2><ol class=steps><li>Stable, predictable cash flows to service and repay debt.</li><li>Low capex and working capital needs, so more cash flow goes to debt paydown.</li><li>Strong market position and defensible margins that hold up in a downturn.</li><li>Operational upside: cost savings, pricing, growth or add-on acquisitions.</li><li>Sensible entry price, limited existing debt, a capable management team and a credible exit route.</li></ol>
<h2>Sample answer</h2><div class=sample><p class=ans>In an LBO the buyer funds a large part of the price with debt, so the first thing I look for is cash flow that can support that debt: stable, recurring revenue and predictable margins. A business with long-term contracts or subscription revenue is better than one whose earnings swing with commodity prices.</p><p class=ans>Second, the cash has to be available. Low capital expenditure and modest working capital needs mean more free cash flow goes to paying down debt, which builds equity value. A strong market position helps margins hold up in a recession, when the debt is most dangerous. Hard assets can also help, since lenders lend more against collateral.</p><p class=ans>Third, there should be room to improve. Private equity returns come from growing EBITDA, so I want identifiable levers: cost savings, pricing, expansion or add-on acquisitions in a fragmented market. And the deal has to make sense financially: a reasonable entry multiple, a management team that can execute, and a believable exit, whether a sale to a strategic buyer, another sponsor or an IPO, after a typical holding period of several years.</p></div>
<h2>Likely follow-ups</h2><div class=fu><b>Why is a high-growth tech company with heavy cash burn a poor LBO candidate?</b> It cannot service debt from current cash flow, so leverage adds default risk without the paydown that drives returns.</div>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2><ul class=mist><li>Listing only high growth, ignoring cash flow stability.</li><li>Forgetting capex and working capital needs.</li><li>Ignoring entry valuation and exit options.</li><li>Not linking characteristics back to debt capacity.</li></ul>
