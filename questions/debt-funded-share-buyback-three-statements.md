<h2>What the interviewer is testing</h2><p>Whether you can handle a pure financing transaction on day one, then trace the interest cost later and reason about EPS.</p>
<h2>Answer framework</h2><ol class=steps><li>Day one income statement: no change.</li><li>Cash flow statement: cash from financing +$100M from new debt and -$100M for the repurchase, so net cash is unchanged.</li><li>Balance sheet: debt up $100M; equity down $100M through treasury stock; assets unchanged.</li><li>Afterwards: at 6% interest, interest expense is $6M, net income falls $4.5M at 25% tax, and cash and retained earnings fall $4.5M.</li><li>EPS: accretive if the after-tax cost of debt (4.5%) is below the company&#x27;s earnings yield (E/P), since the share count falls.</li></ol>
<h2>Sample answer</h2><div class=sample><p class=ans>On the day of the transaction, nothing hits the income statement. On the cash flow statement, both legs sit in financing activities: issuing debt brings in $100M and repurchasing shares uses $100M, so net change in cash is zero. On the balance sheet, assets are unchanged, debt rises by $100M, and shareholders&#x27; equity falls by $100M through treasury stock. Liabilities plus equity are unchanged, so it balances.</p><p class=ans>Over the following year, the company pays interest. If the rate is 6%, interest expense is $6M, pre-tax income falls by $6M, and net income falls by $4.5M at a 25% tax rate. Cash falls by $4.5M through the cash flow statement, and retained earnings fall by $4.5M on the balance sheet.</p><p class=ans>The interesting question is EPS. Net income is lower, but there are fewer shares. The buyback is accretive if the after-tax cost of debt, here 4.5%, is lower than the earnings yield on the shares repurchased, which is earnings per share divided by the share price. A company trading at 15 times earnings has an earnings yield of about 6.7%, so this buyback would raise EPS.</p></div>
<h2>Likely follow-ups</h2><div class=fu><b>Does the buyback change enterprise value?</b> In the simple view no: equity value falls by $100M and debt rises by $100M; in practice taxes, signaling and distress risk can shift it.</div>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2><ul class=mist><li>Showing the repurchase as an expense on the income statement.</li><li>Putting the repurchase in investing activities.</li><li>Forgetting the interest cost in later periods.</li><li>Assuming every buyback raises EPS without comparing yields.</li></ul>
