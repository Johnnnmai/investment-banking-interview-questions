<h2>What the interviewer is testing</h2><p>Whether you know the steps of an accretion/dilution analysis, the adjustments that matter, and the quick P/E rule for all-stock deals.</p>
<h2>Answer framework</h2><ol class=steps><li>Set the purchase price and form of consideration (cash, stock, debt).</li><li>Project both companies&#x27; income statements.</li><li>Combine and adjust: foregone interest on cash, interest on new debt, new shares issued, amortization of new intangibles, synergies.</li><li>Compute pro forma EPS and compare with the buyer&#x27;s standalone EPS.</li><li>Mention the fuller version: pro forma balance sheet with purchase price allocation and goodwill.</li></ol>
<h2>Sample answer</h2><div class=sample><p class=ans>I set the purchase price and form of consideration: cash, stock or debt. I project both companies&#x27; income statements, combine them and adjust for foregone interest on cash used, interest on new debt, new shares issued, amortization of new intangibles and synergies.</p><p class=ans>Then I compute pro forma EPS and compare it with the buyer&#x27;s standalone EPS. If pro forma EPS is higher, the deal is accretive; if lower, dilutive. A fuller model also builds the pro forma balance sheet with purchase price allocation and goodwill.</p><p class=ans>Accretion/dilution is a shorthand that markets watch, but it is not the same as value creation; a dilutive deal can still create value and vice versa.</p></div>
<h2>Likely follow-ups</h2><div class=fu><b>In an all-stock deal, what determines accretion or dilution?</b> If the buyer&#x27;s P/E is higher than the P/E it pays for the target, the deal is accretive, ignoring synergies and other adjustments.</div><div class=fu><b>And for a mixed deal?</b> Compare the weighted cost of acquisition (after-tax cost of debt, foregone interest on cash, buyer&#x27;s earnings yield for stock) with the seller&#x27;s earnings yield at the purchase price.</div>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2><ul class=mist><li>Forgetting foregone interest on cash used.</li><li>Leaving out new shares issued in a stock deal.</li><li>Equating accretion with value creation.</li><li>Getting the P/E rule backwards.</li></ul>
