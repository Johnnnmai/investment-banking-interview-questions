<h2>What the interviewer is testing</h2><p>Whether you can explain the EPS test behind deals, list the pro forma adjustments and use the shortcut, while knowing its limits.</p>
<h2>Answer framework</h2><ol class=steps><li>Definition: compare the buyer&#x27;s pro forma EPS after the deal with its standalone EPS; higher is accretive, lower is dilutive.</li><li>Pro forma net income: buyer + target net income + after-tax synergies - after-tax interest on new debt - lost after-tax interest on cash used - after-tax D&amp;A from asset write-ups.</li><li>Pro forma shares: buyer shares + new shares issued.</li><li>Shortcut: compare the target&#x27;s earnings yield at the purchase price with the weighted after-tax cost of the funding.</li><li>Limits: EPS accretion does not mean the deal creates value.</li></ol>
<h2>Sample answer</h2><div class=sample><p class=ans>Accretion or dilution measures whether an acquisition raises or lowers the buyer&#x27;s earnings per share. I calculate pro forma EPS for the combined company and compare it to the buyer&#x27;s standalone EPS. If it goes up, the deal is accretive; if it goes down, dilutive.</p><p class=ans>Pro forma net income starts with both companies&#x27; net income, adds after-tax synergies, then subtracts the after-tax cost of financing: interest on new debt, interest income lost on cash used, and extra depreciation and amortization from writing up the target&#x27;s assets. The share count increases by any new shares issued to the target&#x27;s shareholders.</p><p class=ans>A quick shortcut is to compare yields. The target&#x27;s earnings yield is its net income divided by the purchase price. The cost of each funding source is after-tax interest for debt, the lost after-tax interest yield for cash, and the buyer&#x27;s earnings yield, the inverse of its P/E, for stock. If the target&#x27;s yield is above the weighted cost, the deal is accretive before synergies and write-ups. Accretion is not the same as value creation, though: a buyer can overpay and still show accretion by using cheap debt, so I also look at return on invested capital and intrinsic value.</p></div>
<h2>Likely follow-ups</h2><div class=fu><b>Can a deal be dilutive and still make sense?</b> Yes, if it adds strategic value or growth that a short-term EPS test misses, for example buying a fast-growing but low-earning target.</div>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2><ul class=mist><li>Forgetting the lost interest on cash used.</li><li>Ignoring D&amp;A from asset write-ups.</li><li>Treating accretion as proof the deal creates value.</li><li>Using pre-tax instead of after-tax financing costs.</li></ul>
