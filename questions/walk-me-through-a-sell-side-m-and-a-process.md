<h2>What the interviewer is testing</h2><p>Whether you understand how a sale is run in stages and what analysts produce at each stage.</p>
<h2>Answer framework</h2><ol class=steps><li>Preparation: pitch and mandate, financial model, valuation, teaser, confidential information memorandum (CIM) and buyer list.</li><li>Marketing: contact buyers with the teaser, sign NDAs, send the CIM.</li><li>First round: non-binding indications of interest (IOIs) with a value range; select a shortlist.</li><li>Second round: management presentations, data room diligence, draft purchase agreement, final binding bids.</li><li>Negotiation, signing, regulatory and other approvals, then closing.</li></ol>
<h2>Sample answer</h2><div class=sample><p class=ans>It usually starts once the bank wins the mandate. In preparation, the team builds the financial model and valuation, works with management on the key selling points, drafts a short anonymous teaser and a detailed confidential information memorandum, and builds a list of strategic and financial buyers. As an analyst, much of my time would go into the model, the CIM and the buyer list.</p><p class=ans>Then the bank goes to market. It sends the teaser to potential buyers, those interested sign a non-disclosure agreement and receive the CIM. After a few weeks, buyers submit non-binding indications of interest with a value range and key terms. The seller and bank use those to choose a shortlist for the second round.</p><p class=ans>In the second round, shortlisted buyers meet management, do detailed diligence in a virtual data room and comment on a draft purchase agreement. They then submit final, binding bids with a marked-up agreement and financing details. The seller negotiates with one or two leading bidders, signs the purchase agreement and announces the deal, and closing follows after regulatory and shareholder approvals. A broad auction maximizes competition, while a targeted process with a few buyers protects confidentiality.</p></div>
<h2>Likely follow-ups</h2><div class=fu><b>Why run a two-round auction?</b> It creates competition while limiting the time management spends on detailed diligence to serious bidders.</div>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2><ul class=mist><li>Skipping the preparation phase.</li><li>Confusing non-binding IOIs with binding bids.</li><li>Forgetting regulatory approvals between signing and closing.</li><li>Not mentioning what an analyst actually does in the process.</li></ul>
